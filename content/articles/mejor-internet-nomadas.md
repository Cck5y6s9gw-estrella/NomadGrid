With 115 cities now in the catalog, this ranking answers the question that matters most to anyone working remotely: where's the best connection? We used each country's average fixed broadband speed (source: World Population Review), so the figure is national, not city-specific, which explains something you'll notice right away in the top 10: several cities in the same country share the exact same number.

The top 10

1. Singapore (Singapore) — 407 Mbps
2. Santiago (Chile) — 357 Mbps
3. Dubai (United Arab Emirates) — 356 Mbps
4. Abu Dhabi (United Arab Emirates) — 356 Mbps
5. Paris (France) — 346 Mbps
6. Toulouse (France) — 346 Mbps
7. Strasbourg (France) — 346 Mbps
8. Hong Kong (Hong Kong) — 345 Mbps
9. Reykjavik (Iceland) — 318 Mbps
10. Seattle (United States) — 303 Mbps

Singapore, the undisputed leader

At 407 Mbps, Singapore doesn't just top this ranking: it's the fastest city in Roavio's entire catalog, well ahead of second place. Add a safety score of 7.8/10 and a quality-of-life score of 7.5/10, and it's easy to see why it remains one of Asia's most established tech hubs, even though its cost of living (€1,304/month) puts it among the catalog's most expensive cities.

Santiago, Chile, the top-3 surprise

Second place isn't a European capital or an Asian hub: it's Santiago, Chile, at 357 Mbps, ahead of Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Paris, and Hong Kong. And at a cost of living of just €602/month, far below the rest of the top 5. The one caveat is safety (3.6/10), among the lowest in the top 10, worth weighing if connection speed isn't your only criterion.

Why there are so many ties

Since this is a country-level figure rather than a city-level one, it's normal to see several cities sharing the exact same number: Dubai and Abu Dhabi share the UAE's 356 Mbps, Paris, Toulouse, and Strasbourg share France's 346 Mbps, and Seattle, San Francisco, Miami, New York, and Los Angeles share the US's 303 Mbps. If you're trying to tell apart cities within the same country, internet speed won't be the metric that decides it — cost of living, safety, or quality of life vary far more from city to city within the same country.

Reykjavik: the only top-10 city that also nails quality of life

Reykjavik is the ninth-fastest city (318 Mbps) and, at the same time, has one of the highest quality-of-life scores in the whole catalog (9.9/10, second only to A Coruña). That combination doesn't repeat anywhere else in the top 10: every other city trades off good connectivity against some caveat in safety, cost, or quality of life.

The other end: what you get if internet isn't a priority

At the opposite end of the catalog, Addis Ababa sits at 9.4 Mbps, Suva at 13 Mbps, and Nairobi at 16 Mbps — up to 43 times slower than Singapore. If your work depends on daily video calls or uploading large files, these cities call for a backup plan (an eSIM with data, a coworking space with a dedicated line) before you settle in.

Internet speed is a necessary condition for remote work, but not the only one: seven of the ten fastest cities cost more than €900/month, and some, like Santiago, trade good internet for tighter safety.
