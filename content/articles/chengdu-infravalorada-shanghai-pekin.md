Y sin embargo, con los datos reales del catálogo de Roavio sobre la mesa, Chengdu gana a las dos en casi todas las categorías que importan.

Coste de vida: Chengdu, la más barata de las tres

Chengdu ronda los 431 €/mes sin contar alquiler, frente a los 505 €/mes de Pekín y los 529 €/mes de Shanghái. Es, de las tres, la opción más barata, con una diferencia de hasta el 19% frente a Shanghái.

Seguridad: Chengdu, por delante de las dos

Con un 7,2 sobre 10, Chengdu supera tanto a Shanghái como a Pekín, ambas con un 6,5, según los índices cruzados de Numbeo que usa Roavio. Chengdu tiene fama, dentro de China, de ser una de las ciudades grandes más tranquilas y relajadas del país, y los datos lo confirman.

Calidad de vida: Chengdu también gana

Shanghai and Beijing are, by far, the two Chinese cities most mentioned on any list of remote-work destinations. Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan, barely gets a mention. And yet, with the real data from Roavio's catalog on the table, Chengdu beats both of them in almost every category that matters.

Cost of living: Chengdu, the cheapest of the three

Chengdu runs around €431/month excluding rent, versus €505/month in Beijing and €529/month in Shanghai. It's the cheapest of the three, by as much as 19% compared to Shanghai.

Safety: Chengdu ahead of both

At 7.2 out of 10, Chengdu beats both Shanghai and Beijing, which both sit at 6.5, per the cross-referenced Numbeo indices Roavio uses. Chengdu has a reputation within China for being one of the calmer, more relaxed big cities in the country, and the data backs that up.

Quality of life: Chengdu wins here too

Chengdu scores 7.6 out of 10 on overall quality of life, ahead of Shanghai's 7.1 and notably ahead of Beijing's 6.7, per the cross-referenced Numbeo indices Roavio uses. Less pollution, less traffic, and a more relaxed pace of life explain much of the gap.

Internet: all three tied

Since they share the same national infrastructure, all three cities have the same average internet speed, 223 Mbps, among the highest in the entire catalog.

So why does nobody talk about Chengdu?

Shanghai and Beijing have something Chengdu doesn't: a huge international community already established, more foreign company presence, and, in Shanghai's case, considerably more English-language options. Chengdu demands more adaptation, especially around language.
