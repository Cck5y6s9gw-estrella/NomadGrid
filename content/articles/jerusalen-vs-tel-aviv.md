Jerusalem and Tel Aviv are less than an hour apart by car, but they're almost opposite cities. One is Israel's historic and religious center; the other, its tech and beach capital. We compare the real data for both in Roavio's catalog.

Cost of living: Jerusalem, 12% cheaper

Jerusalem runs around €1,231/month excluding rent, versus €1,400/month in Tel Aviv, 12% less. Tel Aviv is, in fact, one of the most expensive destinations in the entire catalog, while Jerusalem, though not cheap either, comes in somewhat lower.

Safety: nearly identical

At 4.8 out of 10, Jerusalem sits almost even with Tel Aviv's 4.9, per the cross-referenced Numbeo indices Roavio uses. Neither stands out on this front within the catalog, and in both it's worth keeping a close eye on the regional security situation before and during the trip.

Quality of life: Tel Aviv, with a clear edge

Tel Aviv scores 8.5 out of 10 on overall quality of life, notably ahead of Jerusalem's 7.8, per the cross-referenced Numbeo indices Roavio uses. The beach, the climate, and leisure infrastructure built for a younger, more international population explain much of the gap.

Internet: both equally fast

Since they share the same national infrastructure, Jerusalem and Tel Aviv have the same average internet speed, 273 Mbps, among the highest in Roavio's entire catalog.

Beach and vibe: Tel Aviv, no contest

Tel Aviv has miles of Mediterranean beachfront right in the city center and nightlife that never stops. Jerusalem has no beach, and its social atmosphere is notably more conservative, especially on weekends, when much of the city shuts down for Shabbat.

So, Jerusalem or Tel Aviv?

If what you're after is history, cultural weight, and a somewhat lower cost, Jerusalem is the choice. If you prioritize the beach, an open social scene, and an already-established tech community, Tel Aviv remains the safer bet within Israel.
